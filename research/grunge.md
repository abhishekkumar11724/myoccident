# Grunge (1990s)

## Definition
Grunge, as a graphic-design language, is the visual side of the early-1990s Pacific-Northwest music scene and the American "deconstructed" typography that grew up beside it. It is what DIY production looks like when it is chosen on purpose: photocopied flyers, hand-cut paper, tape, staples, ballpoint and marker scrawl, degraded halftones, dirty black ink on cheap stock, and layouts that reject the Swiss grid in favour of overlap, skew and noise. The rule it borrows from punk is that expression beats polish; the rule it adds is that the mess still has to say something.

## Origins & timeline (dates, people, movements, why it looked this way)
- **1976-1980s, punk zines.** *Sniffin' Glue* (London, from July 1976, photocopied) and later *Flipside*, *Slash* (both 1977), *Maximum Rocknroll* and *Cometbus* set the template: cut-and-paste, ransom-note lettering, typewriter text, photocopy on the cheapest paper, because a copy shop was the only press a kid could afford. Influences named by historians: Dada, Fluxus, Situationism.
- **1984-1989, Emigre and the Mac.** Rudy VanderLans and Zuzana Licko publish *Emigre* from Berkeley (1984-2005, 69 issues), one of the first magazines laid out on a Macintosh, in Licko's bitmap faces. Legibility is argued about openly (the "Legibility Wars", 1990-91; Massimo Vignelli calls Emigre "garbage" in 1991).
- **1988, Sub Pop.** Bruce Pavitt and Jonathan Poneman build a brand around a region and a sound; Pavitt's 1988 catalogue copy for Green River's *Dry as a Bone* uses the word "grunge". The Singles Club starts in November 1988 with Nirvana's "Love Buzz". Bleach (1989) is recorded for $606.17; flyers and covers are photocopied because there is no money.
- **1989-1991, Template Gothic.** Barry Deck (CalArts, taught by Edward Fella) draws it in 1989 from a laundromat sign lettered with stencil templates by a "totally naive" sign-maker; Emigre releases it in 1991. It is one of the most defining faces of the 1990s, acquired by MoMA in 2011.
- **1989 onwards, David Carson.** Born 1955; ex-pro surfer, sociology graduate; art directs *Transworld Skateboarding*, then *Beach Culture* (1989, six issues), then *Ray Gun* (launched Nov 1992 by Marvin Scott Jarrett; Carson's last issue #30, Oct 1995). Layouts: overprinted type, rotated columns, cropped photos, text running off the page. Most famous stunt: an interview with Bryan Ferry set entirely in Zapf Dingbats. Books: *The End of Print* (1995), *2nd Sight* (1997).
- **Seattle poster culture.** Art Chantry (b. 1954; art director at *The Rocket* in the 1980s; Sub Pop, Estrus Records) works with X-Acto knife, Xerox and photoset type rather than a computer; MoMA, the Smithsonian and the Louvre have shown his work; AIGA medal 2017.
- **1981-1990s, Neville Brody.** Art director of *The Face* (1981-86) and *Arena* (1987-90); co-founds FontFont (1990), FUSE (1991), Research Studios (1994). Constructed, hand-built letterforms and a punk/Dada/Pop-Art root.
- **1991-1994, mainstream.** *Nevermind* (Sept 1991) makes the scene global; Marc Jacobs shows a grunge collection for Perry Ellis in 1992; Cobain dies in April 1994; the style is absorbed into MTV and advertising and becomes a "look".
- **Why it looked this way:** cost (copiers, cheap ink, scissors), politics (anti-polish, anti-corporate), technology (early desktop publishing, bitmap fonts, low-res scans, generation loss on photocopies) and a deliberate argument against Swiss/International-style neutrality.

## Defining visual traits (bullets, concrete and checkable)
- Layouts that look **pasted up**: scraps of paper at slightly wrong angles (1-3 degrees), overlapping, with tape and staples visible.
- **Torn and ragged edges** with a pale fibre rim; photo corners and tape on every placed object.
- **Photocopy degradation**: crushed blacks, 1-bit-like contrast, toner speckle, white dropout, black copier-lid borders, stray scratch lines, a halftone dot or line screen.
- **Mixed type**: heavy condensed grotesque, typewriter, handwritten marker, a high-contrast serif cut from a newspaper headline, all in one line (ransom-note lettering).
- **Hand marks**: marker circles, underlines, arrows, strike-throughs, stars, notes in the margins.
- **Ink + paper palette**: dirty black, off-white newsprint, one aggressive spot colour (blood red / safety orange). No gradients; texture does the work.
- Type that is **oversized, cropped or overprinted** (Carson), with text boxes rotated against each other.
- **Rubber-stamp** and label-maker graphics (Dymo strips), ticket stubs, tear-off tabs, cassette J-cards, record sleeves: the paper ephemera of the scene.
- Tactile surfaces: coffee rings, creases, folds, adhesive residue, masking tape.

## Typography (historic typefaces; the OFL/free fonts you chose + sizes/weights/pairing)
Historic: Template Gothic (Barry Deck, 1989/91), Dead History (P. Scott Makela), Keedy Sans (Jeffery Keedy), Oakland/Lo-Res (Licko), Zapf Dingbats (Carson), FF Blur/FF Dirty (Brody), typewriter faces, Letraset/Dymo label type.
Free stand-ins (all SIL OFL via fontsource, self-hosted, latin only, 5 files, ~135 KB total):
- **Anton 400** (display, condensed grotesque: the cut-out headline voice; uppercase, tight leading).
- **Special Elite 400** (distressed typewriter: labels, dates, bullet heads).
- **Permanent Marker 400** (marker scrawl: annotations, circled interests, captions).
- **Courier Prime 400** (clean typewriter: body text 16-17px / 1.55, readable on paper).
- **Abril Fatface 400** (newspaper-headline serif: ransom tiles, broadsheet numerals).
Roughness is applied with an SVG `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` filter on short headings only (never on body text), so every heading stays real, selectable text. Ransom-note letters are decorative `aria-hidden` tiles next to a visually-hidden plain-text version.

## Layout & composition (grids, spacing, shape language, ornament)
- One continuous **wall** (dark plaster with ghost posters and staple holes) with **sheets of paper pinned to it**; each sheet has its own tilt, paper stock, tear and tape.
- A loose 12-column grid underneath, deliberately violated by +/-1-3 degree rotations, negative margins and overlaps; text never overlaps text, and body copy always sits wholly on one solid paper surface.
- Section headings are **spray-painted/stencilled on the wall** (chalk on black with a red misregistration offset), body is on paper.
- Shapes: torn polygons (clip-path), tape rectangles with zig-zag ends, ticket stubs with perforations, cassette bodies, index cards.
- Ornament: staples, tape, push pins, marker arrows/circles, rubber stamps, coffee rings, folds, black copier edges, "xerox crop marks".

## Textures, ornaments & imagery (and the photo-treatment recipe + why)
- **Procedural only**: SVG `feTurbulence` tiles for wall grain, paper fibre, toner speckle, coffee rings; CSS gradients for tape and folds; clip-path polygons (seeded at build time) for tears; inline SVG paths for marker circles and arrows.
- **Photo recipe (`recipe.mjs`)**: "xerox on newsprint". Greyscale -> S-curve contrast -> **stochastic threshold** (noise-perturbed, so edges break up like toner) for people, a **dot halftone** for scenery/objects, then toner speckle and white dropout, scratches texture, a copier-lid black edge on some, and a warm newsprint tint (`#17140f` ink on `#ddd5bf`). Faces are protected by a gentler curve in the centre ellipse. Night shots are lifted first; groups use a softer variant. In the browser a **colour spot** (the untouched original) is revealed under the pointer on hover, or whole on tap/Enter.
- Why: the photocopier is the single most characteristic tool of the style; hard-thresholded faces on newsprint are what a 1992 band flyer looked like.

## Motion & interaction (what moves, how, reduced-motion fallback)
- **Nothing animates continuously.** Hover/focus only: sheets nudge a degree or two and lift (150 ms), tape peels slightly, nav labels twitch, the photo colour-spot follows the pointer.
- Scroll-spy highlights the current nav label; no scroll-jacking, no parallax.
- Reduced motion: transitions are removed (`transition: none`), the colour spot still works because it is a state change, not movement.

## UI translation (nav, buttons, cards, forms, footer, timeline in this style)
- **Nav**: a strip of black electrical tape across the top with Dymo-style label-maker labels (white embossed caps), each at its own tiny angle; active label goes red. Horizontally scrollable on phones.
- **Buttons**: torn red label with black stencil text (primary), taped paper scrap (secondary). Focus ring: thick red marker outline.
- **Experience**: record-sleeve tracklists ("Side A / Side B"), one flyer per employer; education as a gig-ticket stub.
- **Case studies**: pinned index cards / flyers on mixed paper stocks, impact numbers as red rubber stamps.
- **Projects**: cassette tapes with a handwritten J-card label.
- **Skills**: ransom-note tiles, grouped under taped marker labels.
- **Beyond code**: scrapbook boards of taped polaroids and photocopies per category.
- **Writing**: drafts on index cards stamped DRAFT.
- **Contact**: a lost-dog-style flyer with **tear-off tabs** (email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub, resume) and a legal-pad order form.
- **Footer**: photocopier colophon.

## Pitfalls & accessibility risks (and mitigation)
- Distressed/rotated text hurts legibility -> only headings are distressed; body is Courier Prime, 16px+, ink on a solid paper tone, rotation <= 1.5 degrees on text blocks.
- Texture over text -> textures sit on a pseudo-element behind the text; paper behind text is a flat colour.
- Red on dark fails contrast -> brighter red `#f0573a` only for large text on the wall; `#b3321e` only on paper.
- Ransom-note letters read badly to screen readers -> tiles are `aria-hidden`; real text is in a visually-hidden span.
- Heavy filters -> SVG filters applied to short headings only, large papers use clip-path and CSS gradients (cheap), no animation.
- Motion -> hover-only transitions, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`; nothing flashes.
- Hover-only colour reveal -> also a button (`aria-pressed`), works with Enter/Space and tap.

## Decisions for this site (what we do / deliberately do NOT do)
Do: a wall of pinned paper; ransom-note name and skills; record/cassette/ticket metaphors for experience/projects/education; scrapbook About with polaroids, marker-circled interests and a clearly joking "side hustle" card; a broadsheet stats box; xerox photo recipe with hover colour spot; everything readable, ink on paper.
Do NOT: use gradients or neon; animate continuously; rotate paragraphs more than ~1.5 degrees; put text over photos; distort body text; use stock "grunge brush" PNGs (all textures are procedural); imitate any band logo, Sub Pop's logo, or Ray Gun's actual layouts.
