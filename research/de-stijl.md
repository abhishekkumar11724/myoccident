# De Stijl / Neoplasticism (1917 - 1931)

## Definition
De Stijl ("The Style") was a Dutch avant-garde group, and the journal it published, that pursued a purified, universal
abstraction called **Neoplasticism** (Mondrian's *Nieuwe Beelding*, "new image-making"). Its whole vocabulary is: straight
horizontal and vertical lines, squares and rectangles, the three primary colours (red, yellow, blue) plus the three
"non-colours" (white, black, grey), balanced *asymmetrically* by opposition rather than mirrored. No curves, no diagonals,
no depth illusion, no ornament. Painting, furniture, architecture, interiors and typography were meant to follow one law.

## Origins & timeline (facts checked against Wikipedia: De Stijl, Neoplasticism, Van Doesburg, Mondrian, Rietveld, Oud, Huszar, Van der Leck, Red and Blue Chair, Schroder House, Architype Van Doesburg)
- Dutch neutrality in WWI cut artists off from Paris after 1914; a small group in Leiden, Voorburg and Laren started talking.
- 1915: Theo van Doesburg (born Christian Kupper, Utrecht, 30 Aug 1883) reviews a Mondrian show and contacts him; he reads Kandinsky in 1913.
  M.H.J. Schoenmaekers' mystical writings (*Het nieuwe wereldbeeld* 1915, *Beginselen der beeldende wiskunde* 1916) give the
  horizontal = earthly / vertical = spiritual idea.
- 1917: the group forms: Van Doesburg, Mondrian (b. Amersfoort 7 Mar 1872), Bart van der Leck, Vilmos Huszar (Hungarian, b. 1884; designs
  the cover of issue 1), J.J.P. Oud (b. 1890, municipal architect of Rotterdam 1918-33), poet Antony Kok. The journal *De Stijl* starts
  (Oct 1917: Mondrian's "De nieuwe beelding in de schilderkunst"). Van Doesburg edits it to the end. Print run only ~300; ~100 members at the peak.
- 1917-18: Gerrit Rietveld (b. Utrecht 24 Jun 1888) designs the Red and Blue Chair (unpainted beech first; colour added around 1918-23, Van der Leck
  suggested bright colour); joins the group 1919.
- Nov 1918: Manifesto I of De Stijl. 1919: Van Doesburg's geometric alphabet (every letter drawn in a square raster of 25 smaller squares,
  built entirely of perpendicular evenly weighted strokes; revived digitally as *Architype Van Doesburg*, The Foundry, 1997).
  1919-20 Mondrian settles on the grid: thin grey lines, many coloured cells (late 1919) -> heavy black lines, fewer cells, more white (1920-21).
- 1920: Mondrian's book *Le Neo-Plasticisme*. 1922: Van Doesburg in Weimar tries to win over the Bauhaus (Gropius refuses him a post, he teaches nearby).
- 1923-24: **Rietveld Schroder House**, Utrecht (Prins Hendriklaan 50), built with client Truus Schroder-Schrader: planes and lines that
  appear to drift past each other, sliding/pivoting partitions on the upper floor, white + grey surfaces, black frames, accents in primary
  colours. UNESCO World Heritage 2000. The only building made fully to De Stijl principles.
- 1924: **the split.** Van Doesburg proposes *Elementarism* (the diagonal is more vital than the horizontal/vertical; dissonance over harmony).
  Mondrian leaves the group. (Mondrian answered with 45-degree "lozenge" canvases that still have only horizontal/vertical lines.)
  We deliberately side with Mondrian: this site has no diagonals.
- 1925: Oud's Cafe De Unie, Rotterdam (colour-block facade with lettering). 1928: Rietveld leaves De Stijl for functionalism.
  1929: Aubette, Strasbourg (Van Doesburg with Sophie Taeuber-Arp and Hans Arp).
- 1930: Mondrian, *Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow* (45 x 45 cm, Kunsthaus Zurich): thick black brushwork, large red field, small blue and yellow,
  lots of white, lines running to the canvas edge.
- 7 Mar 1931: Van Doesburg dies in Davos; the journal's last (memorial) issue appears Jan 1932. Mondrian: Composition 1930s -> double lines;
  *Broadway Boogie-Woogie* (1942-43, MoMA) replaces black lines by strings of coloured squares (colour as line). Mondrian dies New York, 1 Feb 1944.
- Legacy: Bauhaus, International Style, Mies, Eames House, Yves Saint Laurent's 1965 Mondrian day dresses, Google's 2017 Mondrian doodle, every
  "Mondrian" UI grid since. Largest Mondrian holding: Kunstmuseum Den Haag; Rietveld: Centraal Museum Utrecht.

## Defining visual traits (bullets, concrete and checkable)
- ONLY horizontal and vertical black lines; ONLY rectangles. No curves, no diagonals, no rotated type (vertical writing-mode is fine: it is still orthogonal).
- Thick black lines (about 1-2% of the canvas width), flat, even weight; in late works they run off the edge of the canvas.
- Fields of flat colour: red, yellow, blue, plus white (dominant, ~60-70% of the area), grey, black. Colour is sparing and never mixed or shaded.
- Asymmetric balance: one large plane balanced by several small ones; a big white rectangle against a small intense one. Never centred, never mirrored.
- No texture, no gradient, no shadow, no outline on shapes other than the black grid lines.
- Typography: only straight strokes (Van Doesburg 1919 square-raster alphabet; Huszar's lettering; Vander Leck's and Zwart's rectilinear sans; Oud's Cafe De Unie sign), or plain grotesque sans in uppercase.
- 3D: Rietveld's chair/house show the same thing as planes and rods that overlap without merging, each part keeping its own colour; joints are visible.

## Typography
- Historic: Van Doesburg's 1919 alphabet (square raster); Huszar's block letters for the journal masthead; Vander Leck's rectilinear alphabet (Metz & Co, 1930); Piet Zwart's
  1917 rectilinear sans; Oud's Cafe De Unie (1925). In practice most De Stijl printing used a plain grotesque in uppercase with wide spacing.
- Chosen: **display** = hand-built block lettering (SVG, generated in Page.astro from a 5 x 5 raster; every glyph only horizontals and verticals; my own
  letterforms, inspired by Van Doesburg's idea, not his drawings) for the name and the section titles; **text** = **Archivo** (Omnibus-Type, SIL OFL,
  variable wght 100-900 + wdth 62-125): body 400 at 17px, labels uppercase 700 at 11-13px with +0.12em tracking and wdth 112, big numerals 800 / wdth 125.
- Rules: no italics, no letter-spacing on body, ragged-right text, max 62ch measure, numerals tabular for the stat cells.

## Layout & composition (grids, spacing, shape language, ornament)
- The page itself is the painting: a single full-bleed 12-column CSS grid whose background is black and whose `gap` (8px, clamp 5-9px) IS the black line;
  cells are coloured planes or white text fields. Lines run to the viewport edge (Mondrian 1930s).
- Each section is a different composition on the same 12-col system (never symmetrical): spans like 5/7, 3/9, 4/2/6, 8/4. Colour appears in 1-3 cells per section.
- Coloured cells carry big numerals, labels or icons; text lives on white cells at >= 4.5:1.
- Photos are rectangles snapped to the grid with the same black line around them; leftover grid holes are filled with white/grey/primary filler cells so the mosaic is always complete.
- Shape language: right angles only; `border-radius: 0` everywhere (buttons, inputs, dialog, focus rings).
- Ornament: none. Hierarchy comes from line weight, cell size and colour.

## Textures, ornaments & imagery (and the photo-treatment recipe + why)
- No textures. Optional generative "thumbnails": tiny procedural Mondrian compositions used as project thumbnails (seeded by the project name, always orthogonal).
- **Photo recipe, "neoplastic print":** local exposure + levels keep faces readable, median + Kuwahara flatten the photo into large regions; every pixel is then classified into
  the six values. Colour is deliberately RARE (as in the paintings): low-saturation pixels, and skin, become white / grey / black by lightness; only strongly saturated
  areas become a primary (saturated reds/oranges -> red or yellow, blues -> blue, light greens -> yellow, dark greens -> blue, bright sky -> white). A majority filter removes
  speckle so fields read as flat paint, and a thin black contour pass (Sobel on the smoothed image) keeps eyes, glasses, jaw lines and horizons readable like Mondrian's lines.
  PNG output (flat colours band horribly in lossy webp and compress very well as PNG; 15-65KB each). Why: it reads as the style in any thumbnail, and the photo cell sits flush in the grid.
- Variants: hero portraits (finest, most detail kept), people/groups, scenery (blue/grey heavy), night (lifted).

## Motion & interaction (what moves, how, reduced-motion fallback)
- Planes slide in (translate 24px + fade of the coloured fills only, never the text) on first paint, staggered 60ms: "Rietveld planes sliding past each other".
- Hover: nav cells flood with a primary colour; project rows flood the year cell and slide the thumbnail composition by one raster step; photo cells lift a caption tab.
- Case-study mosaic: tile click = tile inverts to black outline, detail panel wipes in from the left edge (clip-path 260ms).
- Scroll-spy: the nav cell of the current section fills red.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: all transitions/animations off, panels switch instantly. No infinite animations anywhere. Nothing flashes.

## UI translation (nav, buttons, cards, forms, footer, timeline in this style)
- Nav: sticky row of cells, each separated by black lines; wordmark cell first (block lettering), then eight text cells; current = red. Mobile: a MENU cell that opens a 2-column grid of cells.
- Buttons: rectangles with 4px black border; primary = red fill + white text; hover = yellow with black text; offset black "slide" (box-shadow 6px 6px 0 #111 is a depth cue - NOT used; instead the button grows a colour bar).
- Cards = grid cells. Stats = big numerals in coloured cells. Experience = true-to-scale span chart (2020-2026 axis) plus a mosaic of bullet cells. Skills = ruled matrix.
- Forms: 4px black borders, square fields, label caps above, submit is a red cell. Focus: black 4px outline plus a yellow 4px halo (visible on every colour).
- Footer: black band split in cells with the flavour line and gallery link.

## Pitfalls & accessibility risks (and mitigation)
- Block-letter SVG headings: provide the real text (visually hidden) inside each heading; the SVG is `aria-hidden`.
- Colour only for meaning: never; the active nav cell also gets a black underline bar and `aria-current`.
- Yellow/white contrast: yellow always takes black text.
- Photo palette crush can lose faces: contour pass + local exposure; check portraits visually.
- Large black gap lines on 320px phones eat space: clamp gap to 5px; collapse to 1-2 columns.
- Layout shifts: grid cells have fixed aspect ratios for photos; images carry width/height.
- Must not look like Bauhaus: no circles, triangles, diagonals, rotated type, halftone, or poster collage.

## Decisions for this site (what we do / deliberately do NOT do)
Do: page = one big black-lined grid; hero modelled on the 1930 red/blue/yellow composition; per-section distinct compositions; block-letter name and headings;
Archivo for text; case studies as a selectable mosaic; experience as a true-to-scale span chart + bullet mosaic; project index as a ruled table with generated Mondrian thumbnails;
photos in a snapped, gap-filled mosaic with a neoplastic six-colour recipe; square UtilityBar; reduced-motion safe.
Do NOT: use any diagonal, curve, circle, gradient, shadow, rotation (except vertical writing mode), Bauhaus poster collage, halftone, Futura-type geometric sans, or stock Mondrian images (all geometry is original).
