# Anti-design (1960s radical design -> 2010s-now web)

## Definition (one paragraph)
Anti-design is the deliberate rejection of the rules that "good design" had settled on: the modular grid, the neutral
typeface, the restrained palette, the polish and "frictionless" look. It is not the absence of design. It is design
used against its own consensus: type is shoved around, scales collide, typefaces that "should not" share a page do,
default HTML shows through, and visible mistakes are left in on purpose. The craft is in keeping the message readable
inside the noise, so the viewer decides the mess was a choice. On the web it is the 2010s-20s backlash against
Dribbble-flavoured SaaS minimalism (white space, soft gradients, rounded cards, one friendly sans), sitting next to
"brutalist web design" and the "ugly web", but louder, more typographic and more colourful than either.

## Origins & timeline (dates, people, movements, why it looked this way)
- 1916-20s **Dada** (Cabaret Voltaire, Zurich; Berlin Dada). Reject "shocking common sense, public opinion, education,
  institutions, museums, good taste" (Marcel Janco's account of the Cabaret Voltaire). Tristan Tzara: "I am against
  systems; the most acceptable system is on principle to have none." Photomontage, chance, found material,
  cut-and-paste lettering are the graphic toolkit later designers borrow. (Wikipedia: Anti-art.)
- 1913-: Duchamp's readymades make *naming* the act; later "detournement" (Situationists) re-uses existing material.
- 1966: **Archizoom Associati** (Florence; Andrea Branzi, Gilberto Corretti, Paolo Deganello, Massimo Morozzi) and
  Superstudio hold "Superarchitettura" (Dec 1966), pushing "radical anti-design": kitsch, Pop and wavy objects (Superonda
  sofa, 1967) aimed at "the critical destruction of functionalist heritage". Group dissolved 1974. (Wikipedia: Archizoom.)
  Italian *Radical design* / *anti-design* of the 60s-70s (Archizoom, Superstudio, Global Tools, Studio Alchimia 1976 under
  Alessandro Guerriero, Alessandro Mendini) is where the term "anti-design" originates (background knowledge; the
  Radical-design and Alchimia pages could not be fetched).
- 1980-81: **Memphis** (Ettore Sottsass; first meeting evening of 6 Dec 1980; Carlton room divider 1981; disbanded
  1987). "A shotgun wedding between Bauhaus and Fisher-Price"; Terence Conran called it "joke junk"; Jasper Morrison felt
  "freed by the total rule-breaking". (Wikipedia: Memphis Group.) Memphis already has its own theme here; anti-design
  borrows only the attitude, not the squiggles.
- 1979-99: **Tibor Kalman** (M&Co. 1979; *Colors* with Oliviero Toscani 1991): vernacular, "bad boy" design as protest
  against the corporate International Style. Cited by Richard Turley as an influence.
- 1992-95: **Ray Gun** (Santa Monica, launched 1992, ended 2000; art director **David Carson** for about three years,
  last issue as AD: Oct 1995). Chaotic, collage-like, "grunge typography"; the Bryan Ferry interview set entirely in
  Zapf Dingbats is the extreme case. (Wikipedia: Ray Gun, David Carson.) Carson's own line is that image choice, cropping
  and type choice make design, not the ability to overlap things.
- 1997: **Experimental Jetset** (Amsterdam; Danny van den Dungen, Marieke Stolk, Erwin Brinkers). Not chaotic, but
  an influential counter-position: one cheap typeface (Helvetica), restrained black/white/red/blue palette, post-punk +
  De Stijl + Provo roots. Anti-*polish* rather than anti-order. (Wikipedia: Experimental Jetset.)
- 2011-14: **Richard Turley at Bloomberg Businessweek** (with editor Josh Tyrangiel). Bold type, irreverent layouts,
  "little regard for grid structure"; covers like "The Hedge Fund Myth" (July 2013, rising green "Perception" arrow vs
  falling red "Reality" arrow). Society of Publication Designers Gold "Magazine of the Year" 2012; Creative Review Studio
  of the Year 2012; George Lois: "the best consistent set of covers in 40 years". (Wikipedia: Richard Turley.) Turned
  editorial anti-design into a mainstream reference for screen designers.
- mid-2010s: **Brutalist Websites** (curated gallery, "a reaction by a younger generation to the lightness, optimism,
  and frivolity of today's web design", marked by "ruggedness and lack of concern to look comfortable or easy").
- 2018-now: "Anti-design" becomes a trend label on Dribbble/Behance/Awwwards and Gen-Z maximalist web studios:
  stacked/overlapping type, huge/tiny scale contrast, mixed typefaces, clashing saturated colour blocks, stickers,
  hand-drawn arrows and circles, marquee tickers, default-browser UI (blue links, checkboxes, tables) restyled wrong.

Why it looked this way: each movement answered a *polished consensus* (Modernist functionalism, the International
Style, corporate SaaS). The visual vocabulary is whatever the consensus forbids.

## Defining visual traits (bullets, concrete and checkable)
- Broken and overlapping grids: elements overlap the gutter, bleed off the viewport, collide on purpose.
- Extreme scale contrast on one line (giant vs tiny type), words cut off at the viewport edge.
- Mixed typefaces that "should not" go together: heavy condensed grotesque + high-fashion serif + mono + bubble + system default.
- Rotated, vertical and stacked type; text over text (multiply / difference blending).
- Clashing, fully saturated colour blocks next to off-white and black; no gradients.
- Raw default HTML shown on purpose: `<a>` in #0000ee/#551a8b, native checkboxes/selects, `<table border>`, `<hr>`,
  directory listings, `<marquee>`-like tickers, "under construction" tape.
- Visible "mistakes": misaligned captions, arrows that point at nothing, comically large buttons, a tiny secondary link.
- Stickers, stamps, handwritten annotations (circles, arrows, underlines) pasted over the layout.
- "Wrong" image treatments: inverted channels, hard duotone, big halftone, sliced offsets, outline-only, cut strips.
- Still a system: each section breaks the grid in a *different, deliberate* way and the reading order stays sane.

## Colour (palette with hex + role of each; combinations to avoid)
| Name | Hex | Role |
|------|-----|------|
| Paper | `#f4f1e8` | Page and body-copy field (solid, high contrast) |
| Ink | `#0a0a0a` | Text, borders, hard shadows |
| Electric blue | `#1a1aff` | Giant type, rule lines, one full-bleed block, links-with-attitude (7:1 on paper) |
| Safety orange | `#ff4500` | Giant type (3.04:1 on paper, large text only), stickers, the big button (black text on it: 6.1:1; never white small text) |
| Acid | `#d7ff1e` | Highlighter, sticky notes, focus ring, the "one more colour" |
| Raw link blue / visited | `#0000ee` / `#551a8b` | Default-browser links in the directory listing and spreadsheet |
Avoid: orange or acid text on paper (under 3:1), blue text on orange (flicker), any gradient, soft shadows. Body copy is
always ink on paper, ink on acid/orange, or paper on blue/ink.

## Typography (historic typefaces; the OFL/free fonts you chose + sizes/weights/pairing)
Historic: Helvetica/Akzidenz (the consensus), Zapf Dingbats (Ray Gun), hand-cut ransom lettering (Dada), Times New
Roman as "unstyled default".
Chosen (all SIL OFL, fontsource latin subsets, ~120 KB total):
- **Anton 400** - gigantic condensed sans for the wordmark, headings, stickers (uppercase, tight leading, negative tracking).
- **Instrument Serif 400 + italic** - the "fashion" serif: body copy (20-22 px), huge italic words inside sentences.
- **Space Mono 400/700** - labels, metadata, tickers, annotations, 11-13 px uppercase.
- **Bagel Fat One 400** - the bubble face, used only for stickers and the "wrong" buttons.
- System **Times New Roman / Arial** for raw-HTML pockets (directory listing, spreadsheet, fieldset).
Scale: ~1:30 between smallest (11 px mono) and largest (30 vw wordmark). Body never below 17 px.

## Layout & composition (grids, spacing, shape language, ornament)
Nominal 12-column grid with a "rules broken" log: every section violates it differently (and a toy, key `G`, overlays the grid):
1. Hero - wordmark wider than the viewport, second line offset and cut off, photo stuck across the overlap.
2. About - one narrow column of copy with giant pull-words hanging outside the page margin; stickers in the gutter.
3. Experience - a spreadsheet (`<table>`) with merged header cells, each job table shifted/over-wide differently.
4. Case studies - a desk of colour-block notes, each on a different span/rotation, titles breaking out of the box.
5. Projects - an Apache-style "Index of /projects" in default fonts inside a heavy frame.
6. Skills - double ticker (opposing directions) + a type-specimen soup where each group has a vertical title.
7. Beyond code - per-category collage boards: one huge tile, tiny stamps, strips, misaligned captions.
8. Writing - "Under construction" hazard sign + 404-style dialog.
9. Contact - oversized stacked links, a comically big button, a form on a blue block.
Shape language: hard rectangles, thick black outlines, hard offset shadows (no blur), starburst/stamp stickers, hazard stripes.

## Textures, ornaments & imagery (and the photo-treatment recipe + why)
No texture. Flat colour, ink, a few SVG stickers/arrows. Photos are treated *wrongly on purpose* but faces stay
recognisable (recipe.mjs picks deterministically per photo from the hash of its id and category):
Each photo = a colour family (blue / orange / acid / channel-swap, rotated by file number) x a form, so a board never repeats:
- **Blue duotone** (shadows `#1a1aff`, highlights paper) - risograph-ish; **orange tri-tone** (ink -> `#ff4500` -> paper); **acid duotone** (blue -> `#d7ff1e`).
- **Channel swap** (R<->B, posterized) - skin goes blue/orange: reads as a colour-management mistake.
- **Big halftone** (blue/orange dots on paper, blue on acid), with adaptive lift so dark photos never collapse to a solid field.
- **Sliced offsets** (vertical strips pushed up/down; the middle band where the face is barely moves).
- **Inverted channels**, **outline-only** (edges on acid) and **cut strips** (strips knocked out to transparent) for non-face photos.
- **Mono** (high-contrast greyscale) for the few photos that CSS blends with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` over colour blocks.
Heroes / portraits use only face-safe treatments (blue duotone, channel swap, big halftone, gentle slices).

## Motion & interaction (what moves, how, reduced-motion fallback)
- CSS marquee tickers (stats in the hero, skills in two opposing rows). Pause on hover and focus-within, a global
  Pause toy (key `M`), and static wrapped lists under `prefers-reduced-motion`. No flashing.
- "Lab" of tiny keyboard toys, all cosmetic: `S` shuffle rotations, `R` reset, `G` show the grid being ignored,
  `B` boring mode (kills rotation/overlap/blend), `M` pause tickers, `F` random fact (from real profile data).
  Also real buttons, so touch/mouse work; ignored while typing in a field.
- Hover: cards straighten and lift on a hard shadow; photos reveal the original colour on hover/focus.
- Nothing animates under reduced motion; shuffle just re-sets static custom properties.

## UI translation (nav, buttons, cards, forms, footer, timeline in this style)
- Nav: a slim top strip of raw default-blue underlined links (visited purple) plus the AK mark.
- Buttons: oversized, flat, hard shadow; the primary (resume) is comically large; secondary is a tiny underlined link.
- Cards: colour-block notes with breaking-out titles. Timeline: spreadsheet rows. Projects: directory listing.
- Form: native-looking inputs, 2 px black borders, huge labels, acid focus ring.
- Footer: giant outline-text sign-off, build note and gallery link.

## Pitfalls & accessibility risks (and mitigation)
- Overlap hiding content -> text never sits under decoration; overlaps are only title-vs-edge, ghost type, photos vs gutter.
- Contrast -> body always solid ink on paper/acid/orange or paper on blue/ink; checker's contrast test is respected.
- Reading order -> DOM order = visual order; no `order:` tricks; rotation capped at +-4 deg on text blocks.
- Motion -> pausable tickers, reduced-motion static, no flashing.
- Rotated/vertical text -> only labels and headings; copy stays horizontal.
- Chaos fatigue -> `B` boring mode and the shared Clean view link.
- Screen readers -> decorative layers `aria-hidden`; wordmark has a real text node in the `<h1>`.

## Decisions for this site (what we do / deliberately do NOT do)
Do: electric blue + safety orange + acid + ink + paper; Anton / Instrument Serif / Space Mono / Bagel Fat One + system default;
hero wordmark bleeding off-screen; a spreadsheet for experience; sticky-note desk for case studies; directory listing
for projects; double ticker for skills; per-category collage boards with wrong photo treatments; hazard-stripe "under
construction" writing section; comically big resume button; a keyboard "Lab" with `S R G B M F`; a joke "second job" line.
Do NOT: reuse Brutalism's concrete greys/system-font look, Grunge's torn paper/tape/dark texture/ransom letters,
Memphis squiggles, Glitch RGB-split text, or any gradient/blur. No fake visitor counters or invented numbers. Never
overlap body copy with decoration.
