# Ukiyo-e (1600s - 1800s, Edo period)

## Definition
Ukiyo-e ("pictures of the floating world") is the Edo-period (1603-1868) genre of Japanese woodblock prints and paintings
made for the city merchant and artisan class (*chonin*): courtesans and actors, sumo wrestlers, folk and warrior tales, and
from the 1830s onwards, landscapes and travel views. A print is a team product: the **artist** draws it, a **carver**
cuts one cherry-wood block per colour plus a black *key block* for the outline, a **printer** rubs each block onto damp
handmade washi with a *baren* (registered by two carved *kento* notches), and a **publisher** pays for and sells it. The look that
results - flat translucent colour fields, bold sumi outlines, graded *bokashi* skies, cartouches and red seals, a limited
pigment palette - is what this theme re-creates.

## Origins & timeline (what happened, why it looked this way)
- 1603: Tokugawa shogunate settles in Edo; a rich urban merchant class wants affordable pictures of its own pleasures.
- 1670s: Hishikawa Moronobu publishes the first ukiyo-e prints in black ink only (*sumizuri-e*), single sheets and books.
- 1720s-40s: hand-tinted (*tan-e*, *beni-e*) then two/three-colour *benizuri-e* (1744). Okumura Masanobu introduces perspective
  and the tall narrow pillar print (*hashira-e*, roughly 12 x 70 cm).
- 1765: Suzuki Harunobu popularises **nishiki-e** ("brocade pictures"): one block per colour, 5-10+ blocks per sheet, registered with
  kento. Full colour becomes standard.
- 1780s-90s: Kiyonaga, then **Utamaro** (large-head beauty portraits, *bijin okubi-e*; sparkling mica grounds), **Sharaku** (ten months
  of actor portraits 1794-95). 1790: censor seals become compulsory on every print.
- 1827: **Kuniyoshi**'s *Suikoden* heroes (tattooed, muscular, huge patterned robes) make him famous; he later paints cats as people.
- c.1830: Imported **Prussian blue** (*bero-ai*, "Berlin indigo") gives printmakers a blue that does not fade. **Hokusai**'s
  *Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji* (c.1830-32) opens with *The Great Wave off Kanagawa* (ōban, ~25 x 37 cm; sold ~8,000 impressions).
- 1833-34: **Hiroshige**'s *Fifty-three Stations of the Tokaido* (55 prints: Nihonbashi + 53 stations + Kyoto) becomes the best-selling
  ukiyo-e series ever; later vertical *One Hundred Famous Views of Edo* (1856-59, 118 sheets).
- 1841-43: Tenpo reforms ban actor/courtesan prints, pushing artists to landscape, birds, flowers.
- 1868+: Meiji modernisation and photography kill the trade; 1860s-90s Japonisme: Van Gogh copies Hiroshige (1887), Whistler,
  Degas, Monet and Art Nouveau absorb flat colour, asymmetry, diagonals, cropping and empty space.
- 1915+: *shin-hanga* revival (Watanabe Shozaburo; Hasui, Yoshida) adds Western light, rain and night; *sosaku-hanga* (1904+).

## Defining visual traits (checkable)
- **Flat colour fields** (no modelling shading), each printed from its own block; colours slightly translucent so overlaps darken.
- **Bold sumi key-line** around every form; line weight modulates; faces reduced to a few strokes (eyebrow, eye, nose hook, small mouth).
- **Bokashi**: hand-graded colour on the block - sky darkening to the top, sea or mountains fading to cream at the horizon.
- **Limited palette**: Prussian/indigo blue, vermilion/red-orange, ochre-yellow, muted moss green, pale pink (beni), grey, with the
  warm cream of unprinted washi as "white". Foam, snow and highlights are unprinted paper.
- **Cartouches**: title boxes (rectangle, scroll, fan, cloud shape) in the upper corners holding series title + print title in vertical script;
  artist signature beside; publisher seal and censor seal along the bottom/side margin; red seals (*hanko*) are *shubun* (red characters) or
  *hakubun* (white characters on red).
- **Composition**: very high horizon or none; big foreground element cropped by the border (Hiroshige); diagonals; flat overlapping planes;
  ground cut by *kasumi* (cloud bands: cream strips with straight edges) so planes read as layers; empty space is intentional.
- **Pattern**: *seigaiha* (overlapping concentric half-circles = waves) and textile prints (Kuniyoshi, Utamaro) fill clothes and water.
- **Print artefacts**: visible washi fibre, wood-grain streaks in flat colour, ink pooling at edges, slight **misregistration**
  (colour shifts 1-2 px off the line), *baren* rub marks, mica glitter, blind embossing (*karazuri*).
- Format: ōban (~25 x 37 cm), **vertical** for people/Edo views, horizontal for the Tokaido; triptychs for epics; pillar prints.

| Warm 2 | ochre / yamabuki | `#d19a3a` (ref `#FFA400`, kuchiba `#D57835`) | road, sun glow, markers |
| Soft pink | beni | `#e0a39a` | dawn sky, cheeks |
| Green | moss | `#6c8a4e` | pines, Hiroshige hills |
Combinations to avoid: pure saturated primaries, neon, pure white or pure black fills, gradients that are not horizontal bands
(bokashi are bands), vermilion text smaller than ~18px on washi (use the darker red or sumi), washi text on vermilion under 4.5:1.

## Typography
Historic: titles/poems in vertical brush calligraphy (*kaisho / gyosho*), blocks carved from hand-written text; movable-type
*mincho* (a Ming-style serif with thin horizontals and thick verticals) in books.
Chosen free fonts (all SIL OFL 1.1, self-hosted, subset with fontTools from the google/fonts repo to keep weight tiny):
- **Shippori Mincho ExtraBold** (800): display - h1/h2/cartouche titles, large numerals. Latin + the handful of kanji used.
- **Zen Old Mincho** 400/700: body (17-18px, line-height 1.7) and bold run-ins. Readable old-style mincho.
- **Yuji Boku** (400): brush-pen accent for pull-quotes and the signature only (letters only - its digit 1 looks like 7).
Japanese characters used are limited to ones I am certain of: 浮世絵 (ukiyo-e), 東海道五十三次 (Tokaido Fifty-three Stations),
江戸, 名所, numerals 一-十, and single-glyph section marks 序 道 作 技 絵 文 便 (preface, road, works, craft, picture, writing, letter).
All are decorative labels next to English text, never the only carrier of meaning.

## Layout & composition
- Content on a **washi sheet** with generous margins; each section is a "print": thin sumi key-line frame, double border, paper
  margin, and a **title cartouche** (a labelled box with a cut corner and a red square kanji tab).
- **Left rail** (desktop) = nav as a column of "stamp" tabs: big kanji + English label; mobile = sticky top strip.
- Hero: ōban-proportion scene (bokashi sky, vermilion sun, Fuji, kasumi bands, seigaiha sea) with the name in the title cartouche
  and the portrait as a tall **hashira-e** (pillar print) with a red hanko stamped across its corner.
- Experience = **Tokaido road**: a wavering river-road with numbered post-station roundels; each job a station.
- Case studies = a **print series**: tall sheets with bokashi header bands, series numerals, impact stamps.
- Projects = **banzuke** (sumo ranking sheet): thick-ruled cells, red year stamps - a deliberately different, denser shape language.
- Skills = hanging **tanzaku** poem slips on a rope.
- Photos = **emaki** (horizontal hand scroll) per category with wooden roller ends, snap scrolling, drag, arrow buttons.
- Spacing scale 4/8/12/16/24/40/72px; max reading width ~64ch; asymmetry preferred to centring.

## Textures, ornaments & imagery (and photo recipe)
- Procedural: washi tile (SVG feTurbulence rendered once to a tiny seamless webp), SVG seigaiha tile, cloud bands, sun disc, Fuji,
  torii, bridge, wave curl, hanko seals with ink-rough filter - all hand-written SVG (original, no licences to track).
- **Photo recipe** (build-time, `src/themes/ukiyo-e/recipe.mjs`), tuned so a thumbnail reads as a printed sheet and faces stay recognisable:
  1. **grade**: auto-levels (1-99%), gamma to a printable mean, light saturation boost; people photos get a *local shadow lift* (dark neighbourhoods only) and a
     *skin-hue lift* so backlit faces do not fall into the navy/black blocks.
  2. **flatten**: two Kuwahara passes (radius 3+2 for faces, 5+3 people, 7+5 scenery).
  3. **block colours**: quantise in Lab to a ~20 pigment palette (washi, straw, yamabuki, ochre, three skin tans, sienna, shu, deep red, beni, asagi,
     light blue, cobalt, indigo, navy, moss, dark green, stone, sumi); portraits and people shots drop the pink/straw/grey entries so skin does not
     fracture. Night shots (mean luma < 0.3) switch to an indigo/sumi palette with lantern ochre and vermilion.
  4. **carved shapes**: mode filter, then *label smoothing* (blur each colour mask, arg-max, dark classes biased so brows/eyes survive) and removal of tiny islands.
  5. **bokashi sky**: flood-fill from the top edge through smooth, light, blue/grey/warm pixels (only when >= 30% of the top edge qualifies; skin-hued
     pixels rejected for people shots), opened/closed to ignore wires and leaks, then painted as a smooth indigo -> asagi -> washi (or indigo -> mauve -> beni -> straw
     for sunsets) gradient that always completes at each column's horizon, with faint brush-streak noise.
  6. **printing**: wavy wood-grain streaks + mottled ink load on the colour layer; **misregistration** by block family (reds shift +2/+1 px, blues -1/0 px, rest fixed)
     which leaves hair-thin paper slivers at family joins, exactly like off-register nishiki-e.
  7. **key block**: sumi outline from the boundaries of a *heavily smoothed* label map (only large shapes get a contour, texture does not), plus a
     difference-of-gaussians pass for fine features (eyes, brows, wires); line weight wanders slowly; lines are suppressed inside clean sky.
  8. **washi**: fibre multiply and a touch of grain. Output: webp q70, 1400 px / 520 px thumbs.
  Result: scenery and sunsets look like Hiroshige bokashi prints; portraits like flat-colour actor prints; group shots keep silhouettes and bright faces but
  tiny backlit faces can still be dark (known limit).

## Motion & interaction
- Seigaiha strip drifts slowly (40s linear), kasumi bands drift in opposite directions (90-140s), sun rises once on load,
  hanko stamps "press" in once (scale 1.25 -> 1, slight rotation), sections ink in with a short fade/translate on scroll.
- Hover: cards lift with a vermilion off-register shadow (misregistration), buttons "press" 2px; emaki drag-to-scroll with arrow keys.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: all drift/rise/stamp animations off, reveals show immediately, smooth scroll off. Nothing flashes.

## UI translation
- Nav: left rail of kanji stamp tabs (desktop); sticky horizontal tab strip (mobile). Active tab = pressed red stamp.
- Buttons: rectangular "label" buttons with 2px sumi border and an offset block shadow; primary = vermilion with washi text (AA at 700 18px
  uses `#a8321f` bg), secondary = washi with indigo text. Focus ring = 3px indigo outline + 2px washi gap.
- Cards: key-line frame + inner hairline, header band with bokashi gradient, corner kanji tab.
- Forms: paper fields with sumi bottom rule; submit = square hanko-style stamp button.
- Timeline: road + post-station roundels; footer = print **colophon** with seal and a small vertical title.

## Pitfalls & accessibility risks
- Vertical/kanji ornaments must not carry meaning -> `aria-hidden` on pure ornament, English label always present.
- Textured backgrounds behind text -> text sits on solid-ish washi cards (>=90% opaque). Vermilion/pale-blue only for large type or non-text.
- Heavy SVG filters -> used only on a few small elements (seals), washi grain is a pre-rendered tile, not a live filter.
- Infinite drift animations are slow, GPU-friendly (transform / background-position on small layers) and disabled for reduced motion.
- Clichés: avoid stereotyped "oriental" font tricks; Latin type is a real serif, kanji are correct and few.

## Decisions for this site (what we do / do NOT do)
Do: washi ground; indigo + vermilion + ochre; hand-written SVG art; cartouche headings; Tokaido road for experience; banzuke for projects;
emaki scrolls for photos; hanko "AK" and a 浮世 seal; woodblock-treated photos with misregistration; strong contrast for all facts.

use gradients that are not band-like, flash anything, load external fonts/CDN, use 3D/glossy effects (flat print only).
