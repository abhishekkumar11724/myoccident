## Definition
Mid-century modern graphic design is the optimistic, post-war visual language of roughly 1945-1969: clean, unornamented modernism (the Bauhaus and International Style that émigrés brought to the US) made warm and playful by Atomic Age and Space Age imagery. It is flat colour in few inks on warm paper, geometric and biomorphic shapes (circles, boomerangs, kidneys, starbursts, atom orbits), grid-based asymmetric layouts with flush-left sans-serif type, and a belief that a single bold symbol can say more than a page of detail. It looks like a corporate annual report, film poster, record sleeve or airline brochure from a design studio in 1962.

Why it looked this way: post-war boom and new materials (moulded plywood, fibreglass, melamine, plastics), the Space Race and atomic optimism, émigré Bauhaus teachers (Albers, Moholy-Nagy), cheap-to-print limited-ink offset and silkscreen (so artists designed with 2-4 flat colours and overprints), and the corporate demand for clear, memorable identities.

## Defining visual traits
- **Flat, limited inks**: 2-4 spot colours on cream paper; overprint creates extra colours; slight misregistration (a coloured shape sits a few pixels off its black outline) is the screen-print signature.
- **Cut-paper symbolism** (Saul Bass): one simplified, angular silhouette (arm, spiral, body parts) as the whole poster; hand-cut, slightly uneven lettering.
- **Minimal realism** (Charley Harper): "I don't see the feathers in the wings, I just count the wings." Subjects reduced to geometry and colour with a few precise details; the picture is a balanced ecosystem.
- **Atomic motifs**: atom-and-orbit diagrams, ball-and-rod "molecule" structures (Nelson Ball Clock), starbursts (8 or 16-point), sputniks (circle with radiating spikes).
- **Biomorphic shapes**: boomerangs, kidneys, amoebas, artist's-palette forms, free-floating parallelograms and "dingbat" ornaments (starburst / boomerang / rectangles).
- **Swiss / International Typographic Style**: mathematical grid, asymmetric balance, flush-left ragged-right sans, objective photography, type as image (Muller-Brockmann, Hofmann, de Harak).
- **Dots and stripes** (Eames, Girard): evenly spaced coloured circles and bars as pattern and as data marks.
- **Colour**: warm - mustard, burnt orange, teal / turquoise, avocado, teak brown, cream, with near-black walnut ink. Atomic turquoise and pink are the Googie extremes; we stay on the warm-earthy side.
- **Wit**: Paul Rand: play, simple geometry, strong contrast of sans and serif; logos that survive being blurred.
- **Information design**: broadsheet bulletins, annual reports with boxed key figures, route maps and airline timetables (Braniff, Swissair).

## Typography
Historic: Futura (Renner 1927; Futura Extra Bold was Kubrick's favourite), Helvetica / Univers / Akzidenz-Grotesk (Swiss style), Bodoni, Clarendon and Century Schoolbook for editorial/annual-report body, Optima (1958, unverified), Eurostile (1962, Novarese, unverified) for space-age tech, hand-cut caps for posters (Bass), script brush for diners.
Chosen (all SIL OFL, self-hosted woff2, 127 KB total):
- **Jost** (variable 100-900; Indestructible Type, Futura-inspired geometric sans) - headings in heavy weights (800), navigation, labels (uppercase, tracked .12em), numerals. The Futura voice of Paul Rand / Bass-era posters.
- **Fraunces** (variable weight, upright + italic; Undercase Type) - warm "wonky" old-style serif: lead paragraphs, pull quotes, the italic surname, drop caps. Plays the Bodoni/Clarendon role Rand paired against Futura.
- **Michroma** (400; Eurostile-like wide techno-sans) - tiny: clock readout, scoreboard figures, mono-ish tags (space-age / instrument-panel voice). Used at >= 12px only.
Scale: h1 `clamp(3.6rem, 9.2vw, 8.4rem)` Jost 800 -0.035em; h2 `clamp(2.2rem, 5.2vw, 4.4rem)`; body 17.5px / 1.6 Jost 400 or Fraunces 400 for long paragraphs; labels 12-13px uppercase tracked. Never body < 15px.

## Layout & composition
- 12-column asymmetric grid (max 1280px), generous outer margins, flush-left text, ragged right; large flat shapes overlap the grid on purpose.
- Page = **"The Annual Report", front page**: dateline bar (volume, live date, live Gurugram time), masthead nav with section "page numbers", headline story (name + portrait in a pebble/kidney frame with a misregistered duplicate), the **Scoreboard** box with the six key numbers, then sections as inside pages.
- Section headers: numbered circle (ink-colour rotates), uppercase Jost title, Fraunces italic deck, a hairline rule - like a magazine department head.
- Colour-block sections (cream, parchment, deep teal, mustard, walnut, orange) provide the "big flat shapes" rhythm; each section a different composition: asymmetric editorial (About), airline itinerary / route line (Experience), film-poster cards (Case studies), Swiss ruled catalogue table (Projects), atomic-orbit "molecule" cards with Eames dots (Skills), Kodachrome slide-mount contact sheets (Beyond code), "next issue" teaser column (Writing), airmail envelope (Contact).
- Shapes: circles, ellipses (orbits), 16-point starbursts, boomerang and kidney blobs, arch windows, parallelogram cut-paper facets. Corners are either dead-square or fully round; no half-hearted 4px radii.

## Textures, ornaments & imagery
- **Paper**: SVG feTurbulence grain (data-URI, static) over the cream; faint fibre speckle.
- **Halftone**: CSS radial-gradient dot screens on large colour shapes (static, masked).
- **Misregistration**: every big shape has a second, offset, multiply-blended shape in another ink.
- **Photo recipe "limited-ink screen print"** (`recipe.mjs`): local-exposure balance + auto levels so backlit faces survive -> split into a **colour plate** (4 flat tonal steps chosen per-pixel from three ink families: warm (ink-brown / burnt orange / mustard / paper), teal (sky/water) and avocado (foliage) by a heavily blurred hue map so there is no speckle) quantised through a rotated **halftone dot screen** -> a separate **key plate** (darkest tones) printed in walnut ink **offset by a few pixels** (misregistration) -> paper fibre texture and light grain. Result: obviously a 3-4 colour poster at thumbnail size, faces keep eyes / glasses / hair detail via the key plate.
- Photos presented as **35mm slide mounts** (Kodachrome era): cream/orange/mustard/teal cardboard frames with rounded corners, a window, a printed slide number; click opens the untouched colour original in a "projector" dialog.
- Ornament: Eames-style dots as list bullets and data marks, starburst "stickers", orbit rings, airmail stripes, tiny atom diagram, "dingbat" stars between sections.

## Motion & interaction
- **Live Gurugram clock** (sunburst / Ball-Clock dial in SVG, hands update each second; digital readout in the dateline) plus an availability line that reads the time of day ("At the desk" / "Off the clock - probably on a motorbike"). No claims about response times.
- Scroll reveals: small translate + fade (once) on posters and section heads; IntersectionObserver; content is visible without JS.
- Orbits in the Skills molecules drift slowly; the hero sunburst turns once a minute. Buttons "press" into their offset ink shadow (transform only, 120ms).
- Active nav item highlighted by IntersectionObserver. Smooth-scroll only when motion is allowed.
- **Reduced motion**: all CSS animations and reveals off, clock hands update without transitions, nothing flashes.

## UI translation
- **Nav**: sticky masthead bar - starburst monogram + "inside this issue" numbered links; active item = filled mustard pill; on phones a horizontally scrolling strip with the same pills.
- **Buttons**: fully round pills in solid ink / orange with a flat offset ink shadow (misregistered plate) that closes on press; outline variant for secondary.
- **Cards**: either poster cards (colour field + cut-paper symbol + title lettering + credit block), or ruled catalogue rows with big year numerals; no drop shadows, no glass.
- **Forms**: fields drawn as ruled lines on an airmail envelope (parchment fill, 2px ink bottom border, label in tracked caps), orange submit pill.
- **Timeline**: airline route line (thick teal vertical line, circular stops, departure / arrival labels); education is the "origin" stop.
- **Footer**: colophon ("Set in Jost, Fraunces and Michroma. Printed in six inks on warm stock.") + gallery link.

## Pitfalls & accessibility risks
- Mustard / orange text on cream is low contrast: use them only as fills; text uses ink or the darkened text variants; verified ratios above.
- Texture overlays can reduce legibility: keep grain at <= 6% opacity and behind text, no texture on small text.
- Decorative SVG (starbursts, orbits, dials) is `aria-hidden`; the clock has a text equivalent in the dateline.
- Posterised photos can lose faces: key plate and local exposure preserve features; the untouched originals are one click away.
- Live clock updating every second must not announce to screen readers (`aria-hidden` on dial, readout is not a live region).
- Overuse of retro clichés (boomerangs everywhere) reads as a diner menu: restrain to a few big shapes per section.
- Horizontal overflow from big decorative shapes: contain with `overflow: clip` on the section, not on `body`.
