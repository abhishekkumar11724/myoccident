# Art Nouveau (c. 1890 - 1910)

## Definition
Art Nouveau ("new art") is the international decorative movement of the Belle Époque, roughly 1890-1910, that tried to
dissolve the border between fine and applied art and to unify architecture, furniture, glass, jewellery, posters and
books into one *Gesamtkunstwerk*. Its signature is the **whiplash line**: a taut, asymmetric S-curve borrowed from plant
stems, used as structure *and* ornament, wrapped around flat decorative colour fields and fine dark outlines. It is
botanical (lilies, irises, vines, ginkgo, peacock feathers, dragonflies), arched (windows, frames, doorways), and calm
rather than loud: dusty, earthy colour on cream paper, with gold. It is the opposite of cold "functional" modernism and
was displaced by Art Deco and modernism after the First World War.

## Origins & timeline
- 1859-77: precursors in Britain - William Morris's repeating florals and Arts and Crafts; Red House (1859); Whistler's Peacock Room (1876-77); Mackmurdo's Wren-essay cover (1883) already uses the flame-like curve.
- 1870s-90s: **Japonisme**. Hiroshige/Hokusai prints reach Paris; Siegfried Bing's journal *Le Japon artistique* runs 1888-91. Flat colour, outline, asymmetry, cropping and empty space become the new grammar (see also `research/ukiyo-e.md`).
- 1872: Viollet-le-Duc's *Entretiens sur l'architecture* ties form to nature and structure; it inspires Horta, Guimard, Gaudi.
- 1884-88: the name appears in the Belgian journal *L'Art moderne* (describing Les XX). Hermann Obrist's *coup de fouet* (whiplash, from the cyclamen stem) embroidery c.1895.
- 1892-93: Victor Horta's **Hotel Tassel**, Brussels - open stair, wrought-iron vegetal railings, floral mosaic floors; the first fully Art Nouveau interior. Paul Hankar's house follows.
- 1893: Aubrey Beardsley's *Salome* drawings and *The Peacock Skirt* - black-and-white masses vs bare paper, Japanese-print influence; the *Yellow Book* (1894).
- 1894-95: **Alphonse Mucha**'s *Gismonda* poster for Sarah Bernhardt (printed 1 Jan 1895) - life-size, delicate pastels, an arch/halo behind the head, ornate top and spare bottom. 1895: Bing opens the Maison de l'Art Nouveau in Paris.
- 1896: **Jugend** magazine (Munich, Georg Hirth) gives *Jugendstil* its name; Otto Eckmann's swans and plant lettering. Eugene Grasset publishes his *Plants and Their Application to Ornament* (1896), a pattern-book of plant studies turned into ornament. Mucha's *The Seasons* (decorative panels, no text) and Zodiac calendar for *La Plume* (1896/97) with its circular halo of zodiac signs.
- 1897: **Vienna Secession** founded 3 April (Klimt president; Moser, Hoffmann, Olbrich); Olbrich's Secession Building 1897-98; magazine *Ver Sacrum*. Klimt later: Beethoven Frieze 1902, The Kiss 1907-08.
- 1895-98: Guimard's Castel Beranger; **1900** Guimard's Paris Metro entrances (commissioned 12 Jan 1900): cast-iron stems, glazed canopies, a custom "Metropolitain" lettering. Same year: **Exposition Universelle** (~50 million visitors) is the style's peak; Tiffany (Favrile glass, patent c.1892), Lalique (dragonfly jewellery), Galle (Nancy school, 1901) show there.
- 1903: Wiener Werkstatte. 1905: Klimt and Moser leave the Secession; the style is already turning geometric (Hoffmann's Stoclet Palace 1905-11).
- c.1905-14: expensive hand-craft cannot be mass-produced; Werkbund (1907) and functionalism take over; Art Deco in the 1920s. Re-appraisal from the late 1960s (MoMA Guimard show 1970).
- Regional names: Jugendstil (DE), Secession (AT/HU), Stile Liberty (IT), Modernisme (Catalonia), Modern Style (UK/Glasgow), Nieuwe Kunst (NL), Skonvirke (DK), Ecole de Nancy (FR).

## Defining visual traits (checkable)
- **Whiplash curves** and S-shaped stems; lines that taper and swell; no straight line is allowed to stay straight for long.
- **Organic motifs**, always stylised not photographic: iris, lily, water-lily, poppy, ginkgo, thistle, orchid; vines, tendrils, seed pods; dragonflies, peacock feathers, swans, butterflies.
- **Arches everywhere**: round and ogee-pointed windows and doors, the halo-arch behind a poster's central subject (Mucha), tall narrow vertical panels (*Seasons*, *Flowers*).
- **Flat colour with a fine dark contour**, almost no modelling; gold used as a flat or gradient fill; mosaic/stained-glass cell patterns.
- **Ornamental borders and cartouches** that frame the content and tie the page together; the image and the lettering share one design.
- **Hand-drawn lettering** designed with the picture - slightly irregular, soft serifs, curved bars (Mucha, Eckmann, Guimard "Metropolitain", Grasset); never neutral type.
- **Hair and drapery turn into ornament** (Mucha) - on this site translated to tendrils and vines; no human figures are drawn.
- Asymmetry in the detail, symmetry in the frame; generous empty areas set against dense passages (Beardsley, Klimt's gold grounds).
- Tall formats, panel series, "decorative documents" (Mucha's *Documents decoratifs*, 72 plates, 1902) - the pattern book is itself a genre.

## Typography
Historic: Mucha's irregular hand lettering; Eckmann-Schrift (1900, Jugend) and Arnold Bocklin (1904); Guimard's
"Metropolitain" sign lettering; Grasset's face (patented 1897, derived from Jenson's 1471 roman); Behrens-Schrift; Victorian
fat faces; Hobo (1910, later). Traits: curved crossbars, bulging stems, swash capitals, tight irregular fit.
Chosen (all SIL OFL 1.1, self-hosted woff2, latin subset, via fontsource/jsdelivr):
- **Macondo Swash Caps** (display: name, section titles, drop caps) - hand-lettered, swash capitals that read as Nouveau poster lettering. Use at >= 2rem, never for paragraphs.
- **Federo** (labels, nav, numerals, buttons, uppercase letter-spaced at 0.08-0.14em) - flared, geometric-organic Nouveau sans; has lining figures good for the stats.
- **Sorts Mill Goudy** regular + italic (body 19-20px / 1.62) - a calm Goudy book serif, period-correct and very readable.
Total ~85 KB. Sizes: h1 clamp(3rem, 7.5vw, 6rem); h2 clamp(2.4rem, 5vw, 3.8rem); h3 1.4-1.7rem; labels 0.78-0.85rem uppercase.

## Layout & composition
- **A bound folio of plates**: a gilt-and-sage "cloth" cover (page background) with a cream paper sheet in the middle; vines grow up the cloth gutters. Each section is a numbered *plate* (Frontispiece, Plates I-VIII) like Grasset's / Mucha's pattern books.
- Centre-axis symmetry for the frame (title, dividers) and asymmetry in the detail (curls, leaves, uneven vines).
- Shape language: round arches and ogee arches, ovals/cameos, circles (Mucha roundel), leaf-shaped buttons (two opposite corners rounded), concave-corner cartouches.
- Spacing: generous (96-120px between plates) with an ornamental divider at each break; narrow reading measure (60-68ch).
- Dense passages (case-study panels) vs empty cream areas, as in Beardsley/Klimt.

## Textures, ornaments & imagery
- Paper: fine fibre noise (SVG `feTurbulence`, low opacity) over cream; cloth: faint linen weave on the green.
- Ornament (all hand-written SVG symbols, no clipart): leaf, tendril curl, bud, iris, lily/tulip, ginkgo, dragonfly, peacock feather, ogee frame, corner spandrel, whiplash divider, monogram.
- **Photo recipe** ("soft painted lithograph"): colour first desaturated ~40%, Kuwahara smoothing (r 3-4) for flat painterly fields, luminance stepped into ~9 soft bands (lithograph stones), a split-tone grade that pushes shadows to aubergine-umber, mids to olive/sage, highlights to cream-gold (blend ~55% so skin stays skin), a fine dark-brown contour from Sobel edges (the Nouveau outline), paper texture, light grain, soft warm vignette. Night shots: teal/aubergine grade. Why: Nouveau posters were colour lithographs - flat stones, soft pastel, dark outline, paper tooth.
- Photo frames in CSS: arch window, cameo oval, circle roundel, keyhole, gilt double-line frame. No stretched crops (`object-fit: cover` + `p.focus`).

## Motion & interaction
- **Vines grow along the page edges as you scroll**: a JS-generated SVG stem per gutter, `stroke-dasharray/offset` tied to scroll progress, leaves/curls/flowers unfurl (scale + opacity) as the stem reaches them. A thin growing stem also underlines the sticky header on every width.
- Hero: roundel ring of text rotates very slowly; whiplash underline draws on load; plates fade/rise on enter; dividers draw when they enter the viewport.
- Hover/focus: leaf buttons tilt and gild; photo frames lift; underline curls in on links.
- **Reduced motion**: no infinite animation, vines fully drawn, no reveal transitions. No-JS: static CSS vine tile fallback, everything visible.
- Nothing flashes.

## Pitfalls & accessibility risks
- Gold on cream fails contrast -> gold only for decoration; text uses dark ochre / ink.
- Swash display font hurts legibility in long strings -> only for headings; real heading text always present, ornaments `aria-hidden`.
- Busy ornament near text -> keep ornament in margins/headers, reading areas flat cream.
- Vines + parallax can be nauseating -> slow, scroll-linked only, disabled under reduced motion.
- Dark teal/aubergine bands: body text cream >= 9:1.
- Photos: heavy filters can destroy faces -> blend gradient grade with original, moderate Kuwahara, thin edges only.
